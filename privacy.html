PettyCash Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 23, 2026
Last updated: November 23, 2026

PettyCash ("the app") is an iPhone app that helps you scan work receipts, track how much your employer owes you, and send a reimbursement report. It is made by Radin Ahmadi, an individual developer in British Columbia, Canada ("I", "me", "the developer").

The short version:

  • Your data is stored on your iPhone. Expenses, receipt scans, reports, payments and settings are stored on your device (and in your own iPhone backups, if you use them). I don't run any servers, and I never receive your data.
  • No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. The app has no developer account system, no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs, no advertising, and no third-party code libraries.
  • Data leaves the app only when you send, share or export it, and only to the places you choose: to your own Google account (if you connect Gmail), to whatever app you pick in Apple Mail or the iOS share sheet, or to wherever you save an export. The one exception is a request to Google to revoke a leftover Google sign-in (see section 4).

2. How the app processes data on your device

  • Text recognition (OCR) runs entirely on your iPhone using Apple's Vision framework. Receipt images are not uploaded for recognition.
  • Calculations, PDF generation, search and duplicate detection all run on your iPhone.
  • Storage protection. The app's database and PDFs are stored in the app's private container and use iOS Data Protection, which encrypts them at rest when your iPhone has a passcode. The Google refresh token is stored in the iOS Keychain with "this device only" access: it is not synced through iCloud Keychain and does not move to another device.
  • App lock (optional). If you turn on the Face ID / passcode lock, iOS performs the check. The app never receives your face data or passcode, only a yes/no result.

4. What leaves your iPhone, and to whom

Nothing leaves your iPhone in the background, with one exception: if the app finds a Google connection left over from an earlier install, or made with a Google client this version no longer uses, it asks Google to revoke it and deletes it from your iPhone, without you tapping anything. That request goes only to Google and contains only the old token. Apart from that, data is sent only when you take one of these actions:

a) Send from your Gmail (optional)

Gmail is the default sending method (Settings → Sending). To use it, you connect your own Google account, during setup, in Settings → Sending or when you first tap Send Report. The app then sends the report from your iPhone directly to Google's Gmail API, using the gmail.send permission. No server of mine is involved. See section 5 for details.

b) Apple Mail or the share sheet

If you send a report with Apple Mail or share a PDF, CSV or backup through the iOS share sheet, the file goes to the app or service you pick (for example Mail, Outlook, Files, AirDrop). That app's own privacy policy then applies. PettyCash does not see what happens to it afterwards.

c) Exported backups and CSV files

Export Backup creates a .pettycash file that contains your settings, all your records, your receipt and report PDFs, and your send history, including the Google address used and Gmail message IDs (but never your Google sign-in). Export CSV creates a spreadsheet of expenses. These files are created only when you ask and go only where you save or share them. PettyCash does not password-protect them, so store them somewhere private.

d) Your iPhone's own backups

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, Apple's backup can include PettyCash's data, as with most apps. Those backups are handled by Apple and by you, under Apple's terms. The Google refresh token is not restored to a different device.

I do not sell, rent, or share your personal information with anyone, and the app contains no advertising or tracking of any kind.

5. Google user data

This section explains exactly how PettyCash accesses, uses, stores and shares Google user data when you connect Gmail to send reports.

What the app requests from Google

When you connect Gmail, Google shows you its own consent screen. PettyCash requests only these permissions (scopes):

ScopeWhat it allowsWhy PettyCash needs it
openid and emailLearn your Google account's email address and confirm it is verifiedTo show you which account is connected and to send the report from that address
https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.sendSend email on your behalf. It cannot read, search, delete or change any email in your mailboxTo send the reimbursement email you prepared, with your report PDF attached, from your own Gmail

PettyCash cannot read your inbox, your contacts, your Drive or anything else in your Google account.

How the app uses Google user data

  • Your email address is shown in the app as the connected account, used as the sender (From) address, used to send you a copy if you turn on Send me a copy, and recorded in your on-device send history so you can see which account sent each report. Google's ID for your account is kept with the connection to recognize the same account later.
  • The gmail.send permission is used only when you tap to send a report. The app sends one email that you reviewed (recipient, CC, subject, message and the report PDF) from your iPhone directly to Google's Gmail API. The sent message then appears in your Gmail "Sent" folder, like any email you send.
  • If you tap Send Test Email to Myself in Settings, the app sends one short test message to your own address.
  • The app does not send any email without your action, and does not use the permission for any other purpose.

How Google user data is stored

  • The refresh token Google issues is stored only in your iPhone's Keychain (this device only, not synced). It is used to obtain short-lived access tokens when you send a report or a test email. Access tokens are kept in memory only.
  • Your email address, Google's ID for your account and the permissions you granted are stored with the connection in the Keychain. Your email address and the Gmail message ID of each report you send are also stored in your send history on your iPhone, and are included in backups you export.
  • No Google user data is ever sent to me or to any server I operate. I have no way to access it.

How Google user data is shared

Google user data is shared only with Google itself, to perform the action you request (signing in, sending your email and disconnecting) or to revoke a leftover connection as described in section 4. The people you send a report to see your email address as the sender, as with any email you send. Google user data is not otherwise shared with, sold to, or transferred to anyone else.

Limited Use disclosure

PettyCash's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide the user-facing feature you chose: sending your reimbursement email (and any test email you ask for) from your own Gmail.
  • Google user data is not used for advertising, including personalized, retargeted or interest-based ads.
  • Google user data is not sold, and is not transferred to data brokers or information resellers.
  • Google user data is not used to develop, improve or train artificial-intelligence or machine-learning models (generalized or otherwise), and is not used to determine credit-worthiness or for lending.
  • No human reads your Google user data. I (the developer) never receive it, so neither I nor anyone else reads it. The app has no mechanism to send it to me.

Disconnecting Google

  • In the app, use Settings → Sending → Disconnect Gmail (or Settings → Erase Data → Erase Everything). The app deletes the token from your iPhone and asks Google to revoke it.
  • You can also revoke PettyCash's access at any time from your Google Account at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions. After you revoke access, the app can no longer send from your Gmail until you connect again.
  • Emails you already sent stay in your Gmail "Sent" folder and in the recipients' mailboxes. Manage or delete them in Gmail.

Google's own handling of your account and email is governed by the Google Privacy Policy.

6. Retention and deletion

Because everything is stored on your iPhone, you control how long it is kept:

  • Edit or delete individual expenses, receipts, reports and payments at any time.
  • Settings → Erase Data offers three options:
    • Erase Reports: reports, their PDFs and send history. Receipts stay.
    • Erase Receipts & Reports: all expenses, receipts, payments and reports. Settings stay.
    • Erase Everything: all data, settings and the Google connection, like a fresh install.
  • Deleting the app removes its data from your iPhone. iOS can keep Keychain items after an app is deleted. If that happens, PettyCash asks Google to revoke any leftover Google connection and deletes it the next time it is installed. To be certain, disconnect Google in the app before you delete it, or revoke access at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Copies outside the app (exported backups, CSV files, shared PDFs, sent emails, iPhone backups) are not removed by the app. Delete them where you saved or sent them.

I keep no copy of your data, so there is nothing for me to delete on my side.

7. Children

PettyCash is a tool for work expenses and is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age of digital consent where you live). I do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and the app does not collect personal information from anyone.

8. Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live, laws such as Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), the EU/UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA) give you rights over personal information an organization holds about you, such as the right to access, correct, delete or port it, to withdraw consent, and to not have it sold or shared.

Because PettyCash stores your information on your device and I do not collect or hold any of it:

  • Access and portability: you can see all your data in the app and export it at any time (Export Backup or Export CSV).
  • Correction: you can edit any record in the app.
  • Deletion: you can delete records, use Settings → Erase, or delete the app (see section 6).
  • Withdrawing consent: you can disconnect Google or revoke access at any time (see section 5), and turn off camera or Face ID permission in iOS Settings.
  • No sale or sharing: I do not sell or share personal information, as those terms are defined under the CCPA, and do not use it for targeted advertising or profiling.

If you contact me to exercise a right, I will answer, but in most cases I will have no information about you to provide other than the email you sent me. If you are in the EU or UK, you also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority. In Canada, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or, in British Columbia, the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC.

If you email me for support, I will receive your email address and whatever you write. I use it only to answer you, I don't add you to any list, and I delete support emails when they are no longer needed (generally within 12 months). Please don't send receipts or financial details unless they are needed to answer your question.

9. Security

  • The app connects only to Google (sign-in and the Gmail API), and only when you connect, use or disconnect Gmail, or to revoke a leftover connection (section 4). All of these connections use HTTPS. I run no servers, so there is nothing of mine for the app to connect to.
  • Google sign-in uses Apple's secure system web sign-in sheet with PKCE. The app never sees your Google password.
  • The app stores no Google password and no OAuth client secret.
  • Data at rest is protected by iOS Data Protection and the Keychain. The optional app lock adds Face ID / passcode protection when the app is opened.

No system is perfectly secure. Your data's protection also depends on your iPhone's passcode, keeping iOS up to date, and how you store exported files.
